# Dust indoors and ventilation: general information

This is general information, not medical advice. For health questions ask a doctor.

## Where indoor dust comes from

Fine particles (PM2.5) get indoors with outdoor air through gaps and open windows. Indoor sources add to it: people and their movement, cleaning that stirs up settled dust, cooking, and printing. After a source stops, the dust level falls by itself as air is exchanged, and faster when a window is open.

## When opening a window helps

Opening a window replaces room air with outdoor air. It lowers the room dust only when the outdoor air is cleaner. The Street kit of the station has no dust sensor, so the platform cannot say whether the outdoor air is cleaner; the temperature and humidity of the street can be compared. A rise of the dust after cleaning is short, and ventilation shortens it.

## What the air conditioner does

An air conditioner changes the temperature and usually the humidity of the room. It does not remove dust unless it has a filter that does. On the charts, the conditioner shows as the temperature and humidity falling together while the dust level stays about the same.

## How to read a spike

A short sharp rise of PM2.5 in the room is usually cleaning or movement of people. A slow fall of PM2.5 together with a fall of the temperature points to an open window. These are hints from the data, not a diagnosis.
