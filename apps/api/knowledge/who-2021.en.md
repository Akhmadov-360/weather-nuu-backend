# WHO air quality guideline levels 2021

In September 2021 the World Health Organization updated its global air quality guidelines. The guideline levels are recommendations for protecting health, not legal limits: each country sets its own standards.

## Particulate matter

| Pollutant | Averaging time | Guideline level |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|
| PM2.5 | annual mean | 5 µg/m³ |
| PM2.5 | 24-hour mean | 15 µg/m³ |
| PM10 | annual mean | 15 µg/m³ |
| PM10 | 24-hour mean | 45 µg/m³ |

The 24-hour levels are the 99th percentile of daily values: they may be exceeded on three or four days a year. The 2005 guidelines were higher: PM2.5 10 µg/m³ a year and 25 µg/m³ a day, PM10 20 and 50 µg/m³.

## Other pollutants

Nitrogen dioxide (NO2): 10 µg/m³ annual, 25 µg/m³ in 24 hours. Ozone (O3): 100 µg/m³ as an 8-hour mean, 60 µg/m³ as a peak-season mean. Sulphur dioxide (SO2): 40 µg/m³ in 24 hours. Carbon monoxide (CO): 4 mg/m³ in 24 hours. The NUUz station measures only PM2.5 and PM10 of these; it has no sensors for nitrogen dioxide, ozone, sulphur dioxide or carbon monoxide.

## How the platform uses the guidelines

The WHO levels are shown only as a reference beside the scale. They are not categories and take no part in the calculation of the AQI, which follows the US EPA scale.
